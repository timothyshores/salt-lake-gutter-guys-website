# Working with Brandon

Read this before writing anything Brandon-facing.

## Who he is

Brandon is the salesperson. He cold-calls local gutter contractors and sells
$500 flat-fee websites. He owns the client relationship start to finish - the
pitch, the demo, the objection handling, the close, and the revision requests
coming back. Tim owns everything technical: build, hosting, lead capture,
automation, delivery.

He is genuinely good at the sales motion and interested in entrepreneurship and
hustling. He is **not technical.** He does not know what Claude Code is, what it
can do, or that a custom build is even an option.

## The GoHighLevel problem

Brandon learned this business from YouTube, and specifically from GoHighLevel
videos. GoHighLevel is his entire mental model of what an agency sells and how
it works. That is not wrong, it is just narrow.

**The consequence:** when Brandon says "we need GoHighLevel," what he actually
means is "we need lead alerts and automated follow-up." Those are the features.
GoHighLevel is just the one vendor he has seen sell them.

**When talking to Brandon about tooling, translate feature-first, never
vendor-first.** Say "instant text to the contractor the second a lead comes in"
before ever naming a platform. If he hears a vendor name first, he prices the
vendor instead of the outcome.

## Why we build custom instead of reselling

Worth him understanding, because it changes what he can afford to promise:

- GoHighLevel runs roughly $97-297/month per agency account, and that cost
  lands before a single client has paid anything.
- The thin custom version - Formspree webhook into Twilio - does the two things
  that matter (text the contractor, auto-text the homeowner back) for a few
  dollars a month.
- Same product from the client's side. Very different margin on ours.
- Tim can build it. That is the actual advantage here and Brandon should know
  he has it, because it means he never has to pad a price to cover a
  subscription we are paying for.

**The point for Brandon: he does not have to sign up for a $200/month platform
to sell this.** Especially not before client #1 has paid. He can sell the
outcome and Tim builds the mechanics.

## Sequencing - do not overbuild

**Nothing beyond the website gets built until a client pays, or gives a deposit
or another concrete signal they intend to.** Verbal interest is not a build
trigger.

The order:

1. **Website. $500 one-time.** This is what Brandon is selling right now. Done
   for both current prospects.
2. **Lead alerts + auto-text back. Roughly $100-150/month.** SMS to the
   contractor the moment a lead submits, plus an instant auto-text to the
   homeowner. Built as one bundle for the first paying client, not before.
3. **Booking links, review automation, Google Business Profile.** Later, and
   only for clients organized enough to actually use them.

Pitch the monthly at the same time as the site, but build it after the money
lands.

## Things Brandon should not say

- Do not promise a client is "licensed and insured" on their site until the
  owner personally confirms it. A false claim there is the client's legal
  liability.
- Do not quote a launch date for SMS automation. Business texting in the US
  requires A2P 10DLC registration, which takes a few days to approve.
- Do not quote a timeline for Google Business Profile work. Separate
  conversation, after the site closes.
